# The Buried Kingdom: art brief (pass 1)

Written 14 November 2026 from research (no reference photos from Rhys). Decided in the course brief: Egyptian ruins
half buried in drifted sand, an invented site (not a copy of Giza, Karnak or Abu Simbel), quiet and grand, no
curses or mummies; the river (1–3), the plateau (4–6), the cliffs (7–9); blazing late morning, hard sun, deep blue
sky, pale stone, short shadows, heat shimmer. Kept apart from Red Mesa: pale gold sand, white and honey limestone,
a green river strip, blue sky, no red rock; ruins at monument scale, played to and through.

## The feel

Discovery in daylight: the moment in 1817 when the sand had been dug down to the colossi's chins. Everything
is sun-bleached and still. The river is the only thing that moves and the only green outside the course: one
band of palms and fields with the desert starting at its edge like a line drawn on the map. Up on the
plateau it is sand, stone and sky.

Keep the sand and stone a little desaturated: at noon pale stone goes white if it is too light or too saturated
(the Reclaim's salmon brick). The stone is a few steps darker than it looks in photos, and the light makes it pale.

## Materials (real sizes)

- **Firm sand**: wind ripples 10–15 cm apart close by (fading out by 40 m), megaripples 1–2 m apart in bands across
  the wind, scoured dark patches 10–40 m, footprints of the dig round the camp.
- **Dunes**: a sharp crest, a smooth windward back and a steeper slip face (the lee side darker), the crest
  streaming a little sand in the wind.
- **Masonry**: courses about 1 m high (pyramid cores) and 0.6–1.2 m (temples), joints dark; the stepped core of a
  pyramid reads as horizontal lines, the casing as a smooth face. Pylon walls lean in about 1 in 8, a cavetto
  cornice along the top, a band of sunk relief (a figure about 6 m tall) on the face, not a texture tiled small.
  Columns in drums about 1.2 m high, bundled papyrus shafts and bud capitals.
- **Cliffs**: thick beds 4–8 m with thin flint bands, talus fans at the foot, varnish streaks down from the rim,
  rock-cut doorways (tombs) here and there at one level.
- **Sand drift**: against every wall and statue a ramp of soft sand up the windward side, hollowed out on the lee.
- **Water**: the river slow, opaque, brown-green near the bank going to sky blue out in the stream; the basin's
  quay wall in masonry with steps down; papyrus and reeds 2–4 m along the edge.

## Vegetation

- **The river strip (1–3)**: date palms in loose groves, many with suckers round the base; a few doum palms (forked
  trunks, fan leaves) as accents; papyrus and reed beds at the water; fields in strips with palms along the field
  edges and canals.
- **The edge of the green**: tamarisk bushes and camelthorn tufts on the firm sand for 50–100 m, then nothing.
- **The plateau (4–6)**: bare. A tuft of dry grass in a hollow, no trees.
- **The wadi (7–9)**: a few acacias (flat umbrella tops) in the floor, shrubs at the foot of the talus.

## Sky, light and weather

- The signature: late morning, the sun high (about 60°) in the east-south-east, hard and white; shadows short and
  dark, but warm bounce off the sand fills them (shade is ochre, not grey).
- Deep blue overhead, a pale band at the horizon, no cloud (or a few high wisps).
- Heat shimmer: a mirage band on the far flat ground (the ground there takes the sky's colour and wobbles slowly),
  the far dune sea softening into the haze; no fog close in, so the pale stone keeps its edges.
- Sand streaming off the dune crests in the light wind from the river.

## Life and details (a few, off the lines)

- The expedition camp at the cliff temple (tents, crates, an awning, a spoil rail with tip carts, ladders against
  a colossus, survey flags).
- On the river: one or two feluccas (lateen sails), egrets and ibis in the reeds, a heron.
- On the plateau: a resting camel or a short caravan far off; kites or a vulture circling high.
- Ruin litter: column drums and blocks half sunk round the temples, a toppled statue's head in the sand.

## Landmarks

- The great pyramid, its top third still cased in white (from 1–6).
- The Sphinx's head over the 5th green (4, 5, 6).
- The colossi in front of the cliff temple (the 8th's terrace, the 9th).
- The river's green line (back from 3, from 4 and 6).
- The gate's two pylon towers (the 8th).

## Avoid

- Red rock or red sand (Red Mesa's). Saturated orange-gold sand (cartoon).
- A gold capstone (no 4th-dynasty pyramidion found was gilded); tourist things; curses, mummies, torches.
- Hieroglyph textures at a small repeat (graph paper); relief at a believable size or not at all.
- A haze that washes the stone white, or glare: exposure kept down so the casing and the sand both hold detail.

## References

- Belzoni's account of Abu Simbel in 1816–17: the colossi buried to the neck, about 12 m of sand over the door
  (the-past.com, "The rescue of Abu Simbel"; Leicester special collections blog) — the half-buried look.
- The Theban Hills behind Deir el-Bahari: limestone cliffs about 300 m high over the temples, described as deep
  ochre (ancient-history-sites.com; Yorkshire Ramblers' Club "A note on the Theban Hills") — the wadi's walls.
- Medinet Habu's second court: painted reliefs kept under later plaster (crooktree.com, Jim Henderson's photos) —
  where paint survives.
- The Great Pyramid's Tura limestone casing: white, polished, the stepped core underneath; no evidence the
  pyramidion was gilded (lumenlearning "Pyramid of Khufu"; funfactz.com) — the cased top.
- Karnak's hypostyle hall: closed-bud papyrus columns about 14 m, the central ones 21 m with 5.4 m capitals
  (digitalkarnak.ucsc.edu; memphis.edu Hypostyle project) — column scale.
- The Colossi of Memnon: two seated quartzite kings about 18 m tall, the faces gone (Wikipedia) — the colossi.
- The Nile valley: a green strip a few km wide that stops dead at the desert, date palms along tracks and canals,
  reeds on the banks (NASA Earth Observatory "Great Bend in the Nile"; Balkan Ecology Project's Luxor notes).
- Assassin's Creed Origins (art director Raphaël Lacoste): strong light against shadow to give depth on flat
  ground (TechRadar), and David Roberts' 1838 lithographs as a colour and light reference (Eurogamer).
