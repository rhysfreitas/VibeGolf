# Emberfell: art brief (pass 1)

Written 11 November 2026 from research (no reference photos from Rhys yet). Decided in the course brief: a real
volcano pushed to the extreme, no fantasy; dusk, an orange sky turning purple with the lava's glow taking over as it
darkens; a lava lake, a lavafall, a live lava river, lightning in the plume.

## The feel

Grand and alive. The course is small against the mountain. The light hands over through the round: low sun raking
the black flows of the shoulder, the reveal at the Notch, then a caldera floor in the rim's shadow, lit from below by
lava. Real places pushed to the limit: Kīlauea's Halemaʻumaʻu lake, Hawaiian kīpuka, Icelandic basalt.

Keep everything a little desaturated except the lava and the greens: the lava is the only thing allowed to be
saturated, and black must never crush to pure black (lies must read).

## Materials

- **Ash** (the ground shader's native): fine noise, wind ripples 0.5–1 m apart, darker damp patches.
- **Fresh pāhoehoe**: ropes 0.3–0.6 m across in lobes 2–5 m wide; glassy glint toward the sun and the lava;
  tumuli (2–4 m domes, cracked on top).
- **ʻAʻā**: clinker 0.2–0.5 m, matte, rusty, rough; its flow fronts stand 2–4 m as ragged walls.
- **Caldera walls**: stacked lava benches every 3–10 m (the history of the lake), basalt columns 0.5–1 m wide on
  the high faces, talus fans at the foot.
- **Lava lake**: dark crust plates 2–8 m with glowing seams 0.2–1 m, a crusted levee at the shore, spatter
  fountains at the margins, the surface creeping toward the outflow.
- **Lava rivers**: a bright central channel with crust rafts drifting down it, levees either side.
- **The lavafall**: a bright core, crusted edges, steam and spatter where it meets the pool.

## Vegetation

- **The shoulder (1–3)**: the kīpuka's ʻōhiʻa forest with red lehua and hāpuʻu tree ferns under it, a step down
  from the flow edge into the trees; ferns and grass in cracks of the older flows; pale lichen on old ʻaʻā.
- **Inside the caldera**: bare, except the course's irrigated turf and moss and ferns round steaming vents.

## Sky, light and weather

- The signature: dusk. The sun about 3° up, behind the western rim from inside the caldera; outside, it rakes the
  shoulder orange.
- The sky graded orange to violet; a few stars high in the east.
- The plume lit orange from below, drifting downwind; lightning flickers inside it now and then.
- Inside: the floor in the rim's shadow, lit warm from below by the lava (the walls, the plume's base, the steam).
- Haze thickens toward the lake.

## Life and details (a few, off the lines)

- White-tailed tropicbirds circling inside the caldera (they really nest in Kīlauea's walls); a Hawaiian hawk over
  the shoulder; nēnē geese on the kīpuka.
- Steam drifting from vents across the course; spatter at the lake's edge; embers.
- The clubhouse: a long, low basalt lodge, lit windows; lanterns along the neck to the island (light pools painted
  in the shader, never real lights).
- Tee markers: polished blocks of each tee's rock (obsidian, basalt, pumice, ash).

## Landmarks

The plume with its lightning (holes 4–9), the lavafall (5, 6), the clubhouse island (5–9), the cinder cone (6–9).
The reveal is the Crown's tee.

## Avoid

Dragons, temples, ruins (no fantasy); neon orange over everything; pure black; smoke over the lines of play; more
than the two real lights.

## Rhys's picks (11 November 2026)

- **Rock and ground: Hawaiian.** Glossy black ropy pāhoehoe with a blue sheen, rusty ʻaʻā rubble, warm grey ash, red
  cinder, the walls in layered lava benches.
- **Plants: the kīpuka forest and a bare caldera.** ʻŌhiʻa with red lehua and hāpuʻu tree ferns on the 1st, ferns in
  the cracks of the older flows on the shoulder; inside, only the course's turf and ferns round the steaming vents.
- **Light: fades through the round.** The low orange sun raking the shoulder on 1–3, the sun on the rim at the Notch
  (4), the sun just down and a violet, lava-lit dusk inside the caldera (5–9).
- **Details: lightning in the plume** (not the birds, the nēnē or the lanterns).

## What pass 1 built

- main.js: the dusk deepens hole by hole (`emberStage`: 0 the shoulder, 1 the Notch, 2 the caldera; the sun at 7°,
  2.2° and 0.4°, its colour, the sky light, the haze and the exposure for each); the ground shader's `EMBER` block
  (ash with wind ripples and damp patches; pāhoehoe lobes with ropes round them, dark seams and gun-metal blue patches,
  glossy through `emberGloss` lowering the roughness; ʻaʻā clinker rusting with pale lichen; red scoria; the walls as
  lava benches about 6 m thick, each its own shade, now and then an oxidised layer, a dark line at each bench's foot,
  columns jointed down some, pale streaks); muted turf; `emberFarMat` (the ground shader on the far mountain, its paint
  from vertex colours, so the caldera walls far off get their benches too); ʻōhiʻa left out of `buildTrees`.
- ember-world.js: a dusk dome over THREE.Sky (violet climbing as the round goes on, a magenta band, the earth's shadow
  and the pink belt of Venus opposite the sun); a new lava texture (crust plates, glowing seams, finer cracks, molten
  patches) brighter at the margins and in the rivers, crusting over in the middle of the lake (`intoLava`); spatter
  thrown up at the banks; the lavafall with a bright core and crusted edges; lightning in the plume every few seconds
  (a forked bolt flickering two or three times, a flash, the ash lit violet-white); the ʻōhiʻa (stems, clumpy
  crowns, red blossom) and hāpuʻu tree ferns; fern rosettes in the old flows' cracks on the shoulder and round the
  vents.
