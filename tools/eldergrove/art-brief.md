# Eldergrove: art brief (art pass 1)

Rhys has no reference photos for this course, so this brief is researched: the old-growth coast redwoods of
northern California (Prairie Creek, Jedediah Smith, Del Norte, Muir Woods) for the trees, the floor and the
light, and the Hoh rain forest on the Olympic Peninsula for moss, nurse logs and hanging clubmoss. The mood is
the brief's ("something like Du Weldenvarden", taken and made our own): an ancient endless forest, a few things
grown from living trees, one colossal tree at the heart.

## The feel

Hushed and cathedral-like, cool and green in the shade, with warm gold light coming down in shafts through the
mist. Old, still, alive: everything is growing on something that grew before it. Never spooky, never a theme
park; the grown things (the stair, the root bridge, the hall's doors) are hints, not buildings.

## Palette

Big surfaces first. Slightly desaturated: vertex tones multiply textures, and the low gold sun saturates.

## Materials

- **Trunks**: coast redwood bark: thick, fibrous, deeply furrowed into broad scaly ridges running up the trunk,
  red-brown, cinnamon where fresh. A canvas texture, ridges about 0.6 m apart round the trunk and a repeat of
  about 8 m up it, so a 10 m trunk shows about fifty ridges. Moss climbs the shaded side and the root flare (to
  about 6 m), pale lichen spots higher up, a burl or two low on some trunks, dead branch stubs below the crown.
- **Crowns**: the tiers stay; each bough tier gets a needle-spray texture (dark green, the tips lighter) and its
  underside darker, so from the floor the roof reads as foliage, not facets.
- **The forest floor** (ground shader, `GROVE`): rust needle duff with twigs and cones, patches of sorrel (a
  clover-like carpet), moss on every mound and around trunk feet, darker and damp in hollows. Pattern scales:
  sorrel patches 2-6 m, moss patches 3-10 m, twigs at a few tens of centimetres close to the camera only.
- **Fallen wood** (the fallen giant, the stump's top, nurse logs): bark flanks with the same ridges, a moss
  blanket on top, ferns and saplings growing out of it, broken ends splintered and cinnamon.
- **The hollow stump**: bark outside, red-brown rot inside (punky and paler high up, dark and damp low down),
  bracket fungi on the outside, moss and ferns on the break.
- **Water**: the pool black and still, a mirror; the brook clear and tea-brown over pale gravel.

## Vegetation

- **Sword ferns**, knee to waist high, in clumps everywhere off the mown ground: thickest along the brook,
  round trunk feet and in hollows; thin on the open floor. Kept 4 m off anything mown, as on the Lost Valley.
- **Redwood sorrel** carpets in the shade, in the ground shader (no geometry).
- **Understory**, sparse: huckleberry and rhododendron shrubs (a few pink blossoms), vine maple near the brook.
- **Saplings** on the fallen logs and the stump's top (nurse logs), and young redwoods in the gaps.
- **Hanging moss**: on dead branch stubs and limbs close to the play (the Stair's limbs, the stair tree).

## Sky, light and weather

The signature: misty morning, calm, haze, cool; the sun about 32° up, warm, coming through the gaps in the roof.
The shade is deep green, the patches of sun gold and bright, the shafts long and soft in the mist. The sky
shows only in slots over the fairways and over the Glade and the pool, pale and bright.

## Life and details

A few, never busy, off the lines of play: Roosevelt elk grazing in the meadow and the Glade; ravens and jays
crossing; dust and drifting seeds turning in the shafts; bracket fungi and fallen branches; a soft glow from
the hall's doors in the heart tree.

## Landmarks

The heart tree (the 9th's reveal, glimpsed from 3, 4 and 5); the Glade's light; the fallen giant's root plate;
the hollow stump.

## Avoid

Cartoon saturation (lime ferns, orange trunks); fantasy glow everywhere (only the hall's doors glow); fog so
thick the next tree vanishes; anything in the line of play that isn't a tree; ferns on the mown ground.

  https://www.michaelfrye.com/2025/06/15/bathing-in-the-forest-light/); deep shade with very bright patches where
  a beam reaches the floor (G Dan Mitchell, Muir Woods: https://gdanmitchell.com/?p=5323).

  forests: https://home.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/temperate-rain-forests.htm).
- Hollow trunks ("goosepens") from damage and decay, and nurse logs among redwoods (M. D. Vaden:
