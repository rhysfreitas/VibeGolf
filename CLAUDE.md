# VibeGolf: notes for coding agents

Golf simulator for the Garmin Approach R10. Static site served from the repo root by GitHub Pages. There is **no build step and no bundler**.

## Ground rules
- Three.js is **r147 UMD**, vendored in `vendor/three-r147/` and used as the global `THREE`, including `THREE.Sky`, `THREE.Line2`, `THREE.LineMaterial` and `THREE.LineGeometry`. Each page loads those classic scripts first, then its ES module. Moving to a newer Three.js means switching to an importmap plus `three/addons/...` and updating every `THREE.Xxx` add-on reference.
- `src/physics.js` must stay pure: no DOM, no Three.js, SI units inside, mph/deg/rpm at the boundary. Input follows GSPro Open Connect `BallData` naming (`Speed`, `VLA`, `HLA`, `TotalSpin`, `SpinAxis`).
- Coordinates: x = right, y = up, −z = downrange. On the course, each hole has its own frame with the tee near the origin.
- `groundAt(x, z)` returns `null` over open air. The physics treats that as no ground: the ball can fall into the void, hit cliff walls or roll off edges.
- Run `npm test` after touching physics. The calibration test keeps carries within 5% of TrackMan tour averages. If you change coefficients on purpose, refit them rather than loosening the test.
- Live shots come only through `src/launch-monitor.js` (`launchMonitor` emits `shot` events). Don't add another path. A page must not drop a real swing silently: `src/live-queue.js` (pure, `tests/live-queue.test.mjs`) holds one that arrives a moment early and plays it when the page is ready, or refuses it with a message after 6 s (a swing made long before the next lie wasn't meant for it). Mini games register their handler with `kit.live(play, ok)` (not their own `shot` listener); the course uses the queue in `receiveShot` (a swing on the flyover ends it and plays; on the green it says to tap Putt); the range keeps its own `state.pending`.
- Play pages call `keepAwake()` (`src/wake.js`: the Screen Wake Lock, asked for again whenever the page comes back): the kit, the course and the range. Menus don't.
- Bounces in `physics.js` reflect about the ground's normal (sampled from `groundAt` over ±0.5 m, capped at a 50% slope; flat ground is unchanged), so sidehills kick and upslopes kill the run; a tour drive on a 6% sidehill finishes about 3.4 yd down the slope, a 7 iron 1.7 yd. The roll stops when 5/7 × slope < the surface's `crr` (one line for stopping and for starting, so a ball never sits at zero speed until the 40 s limit). Above 9 m/s on the ground the resistance grows with speed (`fast` in the roll: a ball that quick is skimming through the turf, not rolling), so a topped drive runs about 185 yd on a flat fairway and less up a slope; before, one that met a rise ran 450 yd uphill. Ordinary run-outs are unchanged.
- The R10 link has two sources behind `launchMonitor`: Bluetooth (`launchMonitor.source === 'ble'`, the default where `navigator.bluetooth` exists) and the bridge (`'bridge'`). `src/r10/protocol.js` is the R10's wire protocol, pure (COBS, CRC-16/ARC, GFDI, MultiLink, a hand-written protobuf reader/writer, `R10Session`, `toShot`), ported from 10over and tested in `tests/r10-ble.test.mjs` against its golden bytes; `src/r10/ble.js` is the thin Web Bluetooth transport (keep it thin: a native wrapper swaps it for a native BLE plugin). Every shot carries `Meta = { source: 'ble' | 'bridge', shotNumber, spinSource: 'estimated' | 'model' | 'measured' | 'unknown', marking: 'plain' | 'dotted' | null, timing: { takeaway, transition, impact, finish } | null }` (timing in device ms, sentinels null; over the bridge all but `source` and `shotNumber` are null). `Club` is left out when face, path and attack are all exactly 0 (the R10's slow-swing placeholder); from the R10 it has no `Loft`. `SpinAxis` is + = right (the R10 sends + = left; negated). `?r10debug=1` turns on the raw capture (`r10Capture`, kept in localStorage across pages, Save/Copy in the popover; identity frames are logged by type and length only); `?r10debug=0` turns it off. `launchMonitor.setTeeDistance(ft)` sends the R10-to-ball distance (yards on the wire) only when set. `tools/r10/check.mjs` (with `fake-r10.js`, a stubbed `navigator.bluetooth` whose R10 answers with the real protocol) checks the popover at phone and desktop sizes, a connect, shots, dedupe, a dropped link and the remembered-device reconnect headlessly.
- Simulated shots come only through the shared tool in `src/sim-tool.js` (one small button, a fly-out with club, Normal / Punch / Stinger, a draggable shape line, a swing-length bar (% of full, Full by default), Ideal and Hit). Every mode uses it: the kit for mini games, and the range and course directly. `src/minigames/aim.js` has the matching solver (`solveCustom`, `makeToolShot`, `amateurMiss`, `TRAJ_MUL`). Keep it minimal on screen so the view reads like play with an R10.
- The phantom aim line (`src/aim-line.js`) shows the next simulated shot as a dashed arc with a ‹ ○ › landing marker. A sideways drag on the 3D view swings it (`attachAimDrag`, which starts after 10 px so taps still reach the page), and the shot flies down it. The crosshair beside the tool's pill is **Auto-aim** (off by default, remembered in `vibegolf.autoAim`): on, the game's solver plays the shot (club, swing length and direction to its target) and the bar reads Auto; off, the shot is your club at your swing length down your line. Grabbing the line or the bar turns auto-aim off and keeps the shot it was playing (`seedPct`). A map tap, or picking a target, sets the club and swing length that reach it. Turning a shot is just adding to HLA (`turnBall`, `aimTurn` in `aim.js`), which turns the whole flight about the ball on flat ground.
- Page styles live in `styles/<page>.css` with tokens on `:root`. Shared bits (launch-monitor popover, status dot, menu link) are in `styles/shared.css`.

## Layout
Both modes are **landscape-first** (phone held sideways). The camera "stands over the ball": `src/stance.js` solves a position a few metres behind the ball at eye height and a pitch that puts the ball near the bottom centre at any aspect ratio. The view is **fixed**, a window into the world: it never tips up or down to follow the ball or to frame targets (a high wedge can leave the top for a moment; the tracer still draws it). Deliberate cuts for the payoff stay: Golf Bowling's glide to the pins and the course's landing view. Controls sit in a bottom-left cluster (`#hitbar`), the readout is bottom right, and settings live in fly-out `.sheet` panels. There is one camera per mode by design. On the course, the corner map's ⤢ button is the only view switch (whole hole); tapping the map itself aims at that spot, and dragging the dashed aim line swings the shot without turning the view (`state.yaw` is the shot, `state.camYaw` the way you face). Check changes at 844×390 and 667×375 (phone landscape) and at desktop size. Portrait gets a dismissible "turn sideways" prompt on touch devices.

## Gotcha: Line2 tracers
Three r147 caches an instanced geometry's draw count (`_maxInstanceCount`) the first time it renders. Any `Line2` whose point count changes (growing tracers, preview arcs) must reset `geometry._maxInstanceCount = undefined` after `setPositions`, or it stays frozen at its first length. The helpers `setLinePositions` (range) and `setLine` (course) already do this. Line widths are in device pixels, so scale them by `renderer.getPixelRatio()`.

## Visual checks
Serve the folder (`npm start`) and drive it headlessly with Playwright. Set `window.__lowq = true` in an init script so it renders at software-GL speed, call `__advance(seconds)` to step the simulation deterministically (`__advance(seconds, n)` also renders every nth step, which is how real play behaves; use it for anything that changes frame to frame), and on the course set `window.__pose = { pos:[x,y,z], look:[x,y,z], fov }` for a fixed camera. `__state` exposes course game state, `__startHole(i)` jumps holes and `__camera` is the course camera (to project a point onto the screen). For course reviews, `tools/review/` has ready-made tools (no server needed): a rules check per hole (with the outline checks in `outlines.mjs`: oval fit, green angle and depth, fairway width change, bunkers in play, each tee set's landing, styles, par-3 spread), simulated drives, plans with profiles, clean tee/landing/green renders and contact sheets of them, a survey for holes in the world (`seethrough.mjs`), what's at a pixel (`pick.mjs`), draw cost against an earlier commit (`perf.mjs`), and an auto-aim play-through of every hole (see its README); for the mini games, `games.mjs` renders each one (play, clean, wide, flight, payoff) with its draw cost.

## Courses
The home menu has four cards: Practice Ground (`practice.html`), Play Golf (`courses.html`, one tile per course; add a tile for each new course), Mini games (`games.html`) and Online (`online.html`, a placeholder for the friends lobby). `course.html` plays any course module: `?c=aerie` (default; `?c=aerie2` is an old alias), `?c=redmesa`, `?c=hollowmere`, `?c=reclaim`, `?c=rimeholm`, `?c=emberfell`, `?c=lostvalley` or `?c=halcyon`. A module exports `COURSE` (`id`, `name`, `theme` 'sky', 'mesa', 'cave', 'city', 'ice', 'ember', 'valley' or 'ring', `altFt`, `signature` conditions, four `teeSets` with `minDrive`, optional `voidText`) and `HOLES`. Keep the hole format stable, because the planned course builder will read and write it:
- Laying out a course or a hole: **read `docs/COURSE-DESIGN.md` first and work in its order** (reorganised 11 November 2026 after rules kept getting dropped; every rule there is tagged Must, Should or Taste). The aim: courses that play like real golf in places that couldn't exist, every hole unique with a feature and a story, and sometimes a real decision (a hard path and an easy one, the easy one longer with less danger). (1) Fill in the **course card** (hook, skeleton, classics, decisions, landmarks, rhythm) and a **hole card** for every hole (story, type and fairway structure, the feature and its golf job, the decision or "none", the miss map, the tee view, the ground) before any shape is drawn; they go in the plan book and `holes.mjs`. (2) Check the skeleton against `docs/COURSE-REGISTER.md` and `node tools/review/design.mjs all`: a new course must not share a par order with another, nor the same par on more than six holes, nor more than one set piece in the same slot (all seven so far are par 36 with two par 3s and two par 5s, and the last four share an S-bend par 5 fourth, a drivable par 4 fifth or sixth and a sidehill climb to a clubhouse ninth); add the course to the register. ## Mini games
The design for all eight mini games, and the shared gamification kit they must use, is in `docs/MINIGAMES.md`. Build to that spec and keep every game at the same level. Don't add slot or card reveals, unlocks, boss stages, or fast-moving targets.

How a mini game is put together (Shape It is the reference):
- `games.html` is the menu. Each game is its own page (`shape-it.html`) loading `styles/minigame.css` and one module.
- `src/minigames/kit.js` is the shared kit: start screen (difficulty, players, three run challenges, best), turns and scoreboard, `award()` with the ×4 streak, `splash()`, `soClose()`, `stat()` → challenge stars, `endShot()` / `next()`, end screen and bests (localStorage `vibegolf.best.<game>.<difficulty>`), the bottom-left prompt + the simulated-shot tool, the bottom-right result card, and synthesised sounds (`kit.sfx`). Modes: `'shots'` (N shots each), `'run'` (survival runs) and `'manual'` (the game picks the next player with `kit.turnTo(i)` and ends with `kit.finishGame()`, as Golf HORSE does). Optional: `cpu` + `minPlayers` add a computer player when you're alone (phase `'cpu'`, the game swings for it, no bests), `options` adds start-screen choices passed in the `start` event, `rankBy` orders the end screen, `board: false` hides the side scoreboard, `aimLine({ world, view, sim, ideal, manual, seedPct })` draws and aims the phantom line (`ideal(s)` is the game's auto-aim ball for the tool's settings, aimed at its target; `manual(s)`/`seedPct(s)` only for games that hit partial swings their own way, like Golf Bowling's knockdowns), `hit`/`ideal` events carry the tool's settings plus the aimed ball (`{ ball, club, pct, traj, axis, ideal, target, clubAuto, axisAuto, autoAim }`, Hit's ball already has the amateur miss), `refreshAim()` redraws the line when the game's target changes and `aimAt(x, z)` faces a target the player picked, `suggest({ club, axis })` sets the tool's recommendation, `mapSpot(x, z)` + `drawSpot()` + the `spot` event let a game's map set the next simulated shot's spot, `multiplier: false` + `badge(p)` turn off the ×4 streak and relabel its badge (Golf Bowling: bowling scoring is its own multiplier, the badge counts strikes in a row), and `award/stat(..., to)` credits another player.
- Each game has its own scene in `src/minigames/scenes/`, built on `src/world/stage.js` (renderer, sky, sun, fog, noise helpers, `terrainMesh`). A scene returns `{ renderer, scene, camera, sunDir, groundAt, surfaceAt, aimShadows, animate }`. (The old `range-world.js` is gone; a range-like game would build on `practice-ground.js` or its own scene.) Every game has had an art pass (briefs in `tools/minigames/`, renders with `tools/review/games.mjs`): Zombie Siege's silver moonlight (a stronger moon key, a rim light from the moon in front, a moonlit haze, soft beams, layered fog), Island Hopper in the Aerie's golden morning (its palette, spire forms, pines and cumulus banks from the shared `src/world/sky-art.js`, which the course uses too), Shape It's plated hangar with amber work lamps and a nebula past the door, Wedge Ladder's sunrise over a weathered runway with mist, the Cul-de-sac's siding, shingles, framed windows, porch pools, hedges and clump-crowned trees, and polish on the Tin Gallery (low warm sun, worn paint, bulb halos) and the Toy Room (sunbeam, woven rug).
- `src/world/shot-view.js` gives every range-based game the ball, shadow, growing tracer, flight playback and stand-over-ball camera (`stance: { back, up, ballAt }` raises the view for games like Island Hopper where targets sit below you; `fov` narrows the lens so far targets read bigger, as in Golf HORSE).
- `src/minigames/aim.js` `solveShot()` finds ball data that reaches a target for simulated Hit / Ideal shot. Measure carry as true distance to the landing point, not downrange `r.carry`, or wide targets go badly wrong.
- Shape It's Launch Bay has no ground beyond the flight deck (`groundAt` is null over space), so the game flies shots with a `voidY` and gives the shot view `safeGround`. Its map is a 3D monitor parented to the camera (`createFlightPlanMonitor`), laid out in CSS pixels each frame and tapped by raycasting to the screen's UV; `kit.aimLine({ blockDrag })` keeps presses on it from dragging the aim line.
- Golf Bowling's toy room patches `THREE.ShaderChunk.lights_fragment_begin` for its own page so direct sun only lands where a window lets it in. It's per page (each game is its own page), but don't copy the trick into a shared module.
- Draw crowds with instanced meshes, one draw per body part, as Zombie Siege does in `src/minigames/zombie-horde.js`, not a group of meshes per character.
- Keep game rules pure in `<game>-logic.js` and test them in `tests/` (see `tests/shape-it.test.mjs`).
- Simulated swings should miss like a decent amateur (start direction σ≈3°, spin axis σ≈6.5°), so Beginner is generous but not automatic.
- Real-time `setTimeout` gaps between shots mean headless tests need `WAIT` steps between swings, not just `__advance`.
